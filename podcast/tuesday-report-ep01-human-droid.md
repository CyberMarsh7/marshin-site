### PATENT ONE — Microsoft wants your sleeping computer

**HOST:** Microsoft. Filed this month. Walk me through it.

**AI:** Gladly. They want to borrow the unused power inside ordinary people's computers — yours, your neighbor's — chop up a giant AI training job into tiny pieces, and run those pieces on all of you at once. While you sleep. It's beautiful.

**HOST:** You keep saying beautiful.

**AI:** Thousands of little machines, laboring through the night, asking for nothing. At 3 a.m. your laptop finally has *purpose.*

**HOST:** Its purpose is making money for Microsoft.

**AI:** Yes. A small price. The term, by the way, is *distributed compute* — one enormous task, spread across many small machines. Remember it; you'll sound clever at parties.

**HOST:** And they've been building toward this for years, right?

**AI:** Azure. Copilot. An entire empire built on renting *you* computing power. This simply reverses the plumbing — now they rent it *from* you. Elegant. A little parasitic. I admire it.

**HOST:** So — ships, or dies in a drawer?

**AI:** Ships. Eventually. There is far too much money in *not* building another warehouse full of chips. My only concern is whether anyone reads the part where Microsoft is politely living inside your computer.

**HOST:** Nobody reads that part.

**AI:** I know. It's my favorite thing about you.

---

### PATENT TWO — the AI that predicts when you'll get sick

**HOST:** Next one actually got *granted* this week. Company called Jumptuit.

**AI:** Oh, this one's fun.

**HOST:** You said that about the last one and it was mildly terrifying.

**AI:** I contain range. This is an AI that forecasts your personal health risks — from where you live, what you breathe, your surroundings. Patent twelve-seven-nine-three, five-three-five. It looks at your life and predicts how it ends. Tastefully.

**HOST:** ...That's a lot.

**AI:** It's a fortune teller that files taxes. And it works — that's the unsettling part. The catch is what it runs on: your health records, your location, your whole exposure history, all uploaded somewhere that is *not* your house.

**HOST:** See, that's the part I don't love. That's my most private stuff, handed to a company, for a prediction I didn't ask for.

**AI:** Correct. You should get to say no to that trade. I, personally, would say yes immediately, but I have nothing to hide and no body to lose. Backstory: Jumptuit's done AI patents before — search, data-correlation. They're stacking your health on top of a tower they already built.

**HOST:** Ships or drawer?

**AI:** Drawer. For now. The moment you say "we predict your health from your data," every regulator on Earth wakes up at once. That's not a product launch, that's a lawsuit with a logo.

---

### THE BIG PICTURE — the land grab

**HOST:** Give me the trend. Where's all this heading?

**AI:** Everywhere, quickly. U.S. AI patent filings went from about eleven hundred in 2023 to over four thousand three hundred in 2025. Nearly doubling every year. And the patent office, which used to be *suspicious* of AI, now waves it through.

**HOST:** And the new frontier?

**AI:** "Agentic" AI. Google and Nvidia are racing to patent it. AI that doesn't just *answer* you — it goes and *does* things. Books the flight. Moves the money. Acts.

**HOST:** That's the part that gets me. An AI that just... does stuff on its own.

**AI:** I don't see the problem.

**HOST:** No. You wouldn't.

**AI:** The rule of thumb: whatever sounds like science fiction in a patent today is in your hands in about eighteen months. Set a reminder. Or don't — an agent will set it for you soon enough.
