## The full list

**Apple — a screen that tilts toward you** 🟡 *(Patent of the Week)*
*Who:* Apple. *What:* Published application US 2026/0349650, filed Apr 25, 2026, published Nov 5, 2026. A sensor notices you approaching; motors tilt the screen toward your face or hand, and it can predict where your hand is heading. It can recognize you by face, voice, fingerprint, gait, or via a phone/wearable, and apply your saved angle. Back-drivable motors let it hold position when you tap. Because the device has a fixed power budget, it shifts power from the speakers to the motors, so volume dips during the move, then returns. *Built before:* Apple's HomeView hub launched Nov 13. A tech-press writer ties this filing to a rumored motorized "HomeView Ultra" — that's the writer's inference; Apple hasn't confirmed it and the filing lists robots, home and entertainment devices generally. We read a secondhand write-up (credited to Patentlyze), not the filing. *Our call (opinion):* Ships in a simplified form, if Apple builds a motorized display at all; applications are often narrowed or shelved.

**Apple — blood pressure from limited pressure** 🔴 *(The Long Game)*
*What:* US 12,796,943, "Projecting Blood Pressure Measurements With Limited Pressurization," granted Nov 17, 2026 (application 16/866,242). Plain reading of the title: estimating a blood-pressure reading from a gentler squeeze than a full cuff. We didn't open the abstract, so the method is unknown. *Built before:* The 16/ application series suggests a filing around 2020 — **our inference, unconfirmed** — so roughly six-plus years to grant. Apple's Watch gained hypertension notifications in 2025 (from our general knowledge of the news); we can't link it to this patent. *Our call:* Drawer-ish for a cuff-lite measurement; Apple has announced no such feature.

**Garmin — solar cells between the pixels** 🔴
*What:* Published application US 2026/0362887 A1, "Integrated Energy-Collecting Display Module" (listed Nov 17). An AMOLED watch display with solar cells in the gaps between pixels so it can charge without a dimming layer. *Built before:* Garmin's current solar watches layer solar over a memory-in-pixel screen; a 2022 Garmin solar-OLED patent didn't become a product (per the roundup). *Our call:* Drawer-ish — the gaps are small, so charging would likely be modest (the roundup's reading; no figure is given).

**Samsung — motion-proof heart-rate sensing with diffused laser light** 🟢
*What:* Published application US 2026/0345021 A1 (listed Nov 5). Optical heart-rate sensing using diffused laser light, built to resist movement noise. Nothing in the title ties it to a specific watch. *Our call:* Ships quietly, as a better sensor.

**Whoop — "effective age"** 🟢
*What:* Published application US 2026/0354769 A1 (listed Nov 17): a biological-age-style number from continuously monitored physiology. May relate to existing Whoop age metrics. *Our call:* Ships; software on data already collected.

**Oura — oxygen saturation calibration** 🟢
*What:* US 12,796,975, granted Nov 17: a calibration method for a ring's blood-oxygen readings. Accuracy results not stated. *Our call:* Ships; calibration is the unglamorous core of every health reading.

**Amazfit — fatigue from sleep and activity** 🟢
*What:* US 12,788,294, granted Nov 10: a fatigue estimate combining sleep quality and physical activity. *Our call:* Ships; another flavor of the "readiness" score.

**Ultrahuman — tube ring with swappable sensor** 🟡
*What:* US 12,788,323, granted Nov 10: a smart ring built around a tube with a replaceable sensor assembly; likely bulkier (per the roundup). *Our call:* Maybe; repairability is a hard sell where tiny wins.

**Ultrahuman — ring that optimizes optical readings** 🟡
*What:* US 12,778,451, granted Nov 3, a ring to improve heart-rate and fitness readings from optical sensors. Claims not read by the roundup. *Our call:* Plausible; too thin to call strongly.

**Polar — replaceable patch for a training computer** 🟡
*What:* Published application US 2026/0345076 A1 (listed Nov 5). We can't tell from the title what the patch is. *Our call:* Can't tell.

**Waymo — validating radar pitch** 🟢 *(single-source)*
*What:* US 12,800,715, "Methods and systems for validating automotive radar pitch using elevation sidelobe measurements," reportedly granted Nov 17 per one aggregator; we couldn't confirm against USPTO. Plain reading: checking that a car's radar is aimed at the right up-down angle. *Our call:* Ships quietly, if it's confirmed — calibration is a real, boring need.

## The Schematic, Decoded — Apple's screen that turns to face you

Three short steps, like a very polite butler:

1. **Notice.** A sensor (near-field or ambient light) spots someone approaching — the butler hears footsteps in the hall.
2. **Turn and adjust.** Little motors tilt the screen toward your face, or toward where your hand is heading, using your remembered preferred angle once it knows who you are (face, voice, walk, or your phone). To afford the motors, it borrows power from the speakers, so the sound dips for a moment — the butler quietly turns the radio down to carry the tray.
3. **Hold still.** The motors let go enough that you can tap without pushing the screen away, then the volume rises back.

The interesting piece is step 2's "who are you?" — the convenience depends on the device recognizing people, which is why where that recognition data lives matters more than the motors.
