**PATENT ONE — "Lend out your leftover computer"**

So Microsoft just published a patent — early November — for something kinda wild. The idea: when big AI companies need to train their systems, instead of renting giant warehouses full of chips, they borrow the *idle* power sitting in regular people's computers. Your machine's just sitting there at 2 a.m. doing nothing / and under this patent, it could be doing a slice of the heavy lifting — and maybe paying you a little for it.

Now — here's my honest take. That's clever, but look at the direction it's pointing: big tech wants *into* your machine. They've figured out the power's already in your house. **I've been saying that for a while.** The computer you already own is way more capable than anybody told you. The only question is whether *you* put it to work for you / or somebody else rents it out from under you.

---

**PATENT TWO — AI that predicts your health risk**

Second one — this one actually got *granted*, this week. A company called Jumptuit landed a U.S. patent for an AI system that forecasts your health risks based on your environment — where you live, what you're exposed to, that kind of thing. Patent number twelve-seven-nine-three, five-three-five, if you wanna look it up.

Cool tech. But think about what it runs on: your personal health data, your location, your exposure history — all fed into somebody's cloud. And that's the trade people keep making without thinking about it. **Powerful AI, in exchange for handing over the most private stuff you've got.** It doesn't have to be that trade. More on that in a second.

---

**THE BIG PICTURE — the floodgates are open**

Quick one to zoom out. The patent office spent years being skeptical of AI patents. That's over. U.S. AI patent filings went from around eleven hundred families in 2023 to over forty-three hundred in 2025 — nearly doubling every year. Google and Nvidia are already racing to patent "agentic" AI — AI that doesn't just answer, it *does things* for you. The land grab is on. Which means the stuff that's a research paper today is in your hands in eighteen months.
