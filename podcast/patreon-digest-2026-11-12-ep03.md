## The full list

**Google — glasses that remember what you looked at** 🟢 *(Patent of the Week)*
*Who:* Google. *What:* Smart glasses that save an image when your gaze lingers on something, drop blurry/duplicate frames, and can mask faces, passwords and bystander audio, so you can later ask "where did I leave my keys?" Application filed May 2026, claiming a May 2025 provisional; 22 claims. *Built before:* Google demoed lost-item recall in glasses at its May 2026 developer event, with product plans for this fall (press reports); we couldn't confirm the patent belongs to that product. *Our call:* Ships in some form — the simple version. The "remember everything" version likely runs into other people's privacy.

**Apple — glasses that warn bystanders the camera is on** 🟡
*Who:* Apple. *What:* Recording indicators for smart glasses: a color-changing layer, a glowing wire around a lens, tiny light holes, or a physical camera shutter. Filed April 2026, claiming a May 2025 provisional. *Built before:* Not enough research to say more. *Our call:* The indicator likely ships *if* Apple ships glasses; the glasses themselves are the open question.

**Oura — illness detection from ring temperature data** 🟢 *(The Long Game, in reverse)*
*Who:* Oura. *What:* US 12,778,515, granted Nov 3, 2026 — detecting illness from skin-temperature trends measured by a smart ring. *Built before:* Oura's Symptom Radar feature was a beta in April 2024 and rolled out that December; it grew from Oura's 2020 COVID research (the company reported seeing data changes before symptoms in 76% of cases — their figure). We couldn't confirm the original filing date. *Our call:* Already shipped. The patent is a fence around it.

**Ultrahuman — smart ring optical-sensor patent** 🟢
*Who:* Ultrahuman. *What:* US 12,778,451, granted Nov 3 — a ring built to improve the optical sensor readings of health and fitness data. *Our call:* Ships; Ultrahuman already sells rings.

**Samsung — ring hardware** 🟢
*Who:* Samsung. *What:* US 12,783,008, "Ring-Type Wearable Electronic Device," granted Nov 3 — the physical structure of a ring wearable. *Our call:* Ships; probably tied to its Galaxy Ring line (our inference).

**Ultrahuman — glasses that watch your posture and focus** 🟡
*What:* US 2026/0345067 A1, published Nov 5. Glasses with pressure and motion sensors that read posture and attention, plus blue-light sensors, optionally EEG, and buzz you when you slouch or lose focus. Filed June 2026 with priority from an Indian application of June 2025. *Built before:* Ultrahuman has patented other oddities (a color-changing ring, a UVB sun sensor). *Our call:* Fun, but a pair of glasses is a big leap for a ring company. Slow.

**Samsung — phone assistant that waits on hold for you** 🟢
*What:* An AI that waits on hold, then speaks to the human agent in your voice, firmer as the call drags (three tones: neutral, assertive, satisfied). Filed April 2025; examiner rejected first round in September (common). *Our call:* Ships, in some form. Everybody hates hold music.

**Waymo — spotting a pedestrian or cyclist who starts to fall** 🟢
*What:* Reads body posture points (nose, knees, ankles), flags a sudden height drop plus sharp speed loss. Continuation of a Nov 2024 filing; filed July 2026. *Our call:* Ships quietly inside the driving software.

**Google — chatbots trained to include sponsored items** 🟡
*What:* A reward model predicts user satisfaction with sponsored answers; a second model balances that against predicted clicks. Filed May 2025, awaiting examination. *Our call:* Ships, likely before most people notice. Raises obvious disclosure questions.

**Google — a people-counting sensor that never stores images** 🟢
*What:* A sealed sensor counts people and deletes each frame after processing; only the count leaves the device, with a tamper-resistant housing and public software hash. Continuation of a family tracing to an April 2020 international filing. *Our call:* Ships — "camera that can't keep your face" is an easy sell.

**Sony — a car that releases scents matching ads** 🔴
*What:* Roadside/in-car ad detection triggers a matching scent, with age/gender estimates deciding who gets it. Continuation of a 2019 Japanese application. *Our call:* Drawer.

**Sony — matching second accounts by controller habits** 🟢
*What:* Compares button hold times, gaps, and order to find smurf and shared accounts. Filed April 2025; first-round rejection in September. *Our call:* Ships, quietly.

**Sony — a drone-mounted microphone that stays out of frame** 🟡
*What:* A drone positions a boom mic near an actor outside the camera's shot, remembers positions between takes, and filters its own propeller noise. *Our call:* Slow; niche film-production tool.

**Sony — a tiny rolled-rubber "muscle" actuator** 🟡
*What:* A rolled rubber strip that contracts when voltage is applied; uses include buttons, joysticks, endoscopes. Filed March 2025. *Our call:* Ships inside other things.

**Tesla — a one-cable robot finger** 🟡
*What:* A tendon-like cable drives a two-joint finger; magnet and Hall sensor track angle. Filed March 2025 (international Oct 2023); first-round rejection in October. *Our call:* Hands are the hard part of humanoids; likely ships as a part, slowly.

**Qualcomm — Wi-Fi map of who stood near whom** 🔴
*What:* A phone app maps people's positions via Wi-Fi timing for contact tracing; the filing itself notes privacy concerns. Division of a March 2023 application. *Our call:* Drawer.

**Samsung — a TV that names the background music** 🟢
*What:* Saves audio fingerprints, separates music from dialogue, and sends the song list to your phone. Latest continuation in a family begun with a Korean application in September 2015. *Our call:* Ships; eleven years of lawyer-maintenance suggests it matters to them.

**Samsung — a floating screen that senses your hand** 🟡
*What:* A waveguide and lens project an image in mid-air; a camera through the same plate senses finger distance. Filed June 2026, priority April 2025. *Our call:* Cool demo, slow to market.

**IBM — cars that identify a loud neighbor** 🔴
*What:* Vehicle sensors and a model pinpoint a loud car and a likely cause (worn muffler, brakes). Filed May 2025, awaiting examination. *Our call:* Drawer; who is this for?

**Microsoft — rent out your idle computer** 🟢 *(carried from Ep1)*
*What:* US 2026/0349820 A1, published Nov 5 — split AI training across idle gaming PCs, laptops and phones, with owners' authorization. Coverage notes Microsoft hasn't disclosed product plans. *Our call:* Holds at ships.

**Jumptuit — environmental health-risk forecasting** 🔴 *(carried from Ep1)*
*What:* US 12,793,535, announced Nov 10: AI forecasts of environmental exposure and early health-risk alerts tied to location. Per the company's press release (not verified against USPTO records). *Our call:* A granted patent isn't a product. Holding drawer.

**Autonomix Medical — catheter-based neural sensing** 🟡
*What:* US 12,793,841, announced Nov 10 per the company: medical devices that capture and process physiological signals through a catheter. We couldn't retrieve the full release, so details are thin. *Our call:* Medical devices ship slowly because of clinical trials.

**Trust Stamp — approval for AI-agent transactions** 🟡
*What:* A *provisional* patent (64/195,409, Nov 9) for authorizing transactions started by AI agents. A provisional is a placeholder, not a full grant. Details thin; couldn't retrieve the full release. *Our call:* Needed, but a placeholder.

**Ultrahuman, Polar, Oura & friends — the wearable pile** 🟡
*What:* Polar published applications for warm-up coaching, SpO2 emitter layout, and circadian rhythm adaptation (Oct 29); Oura published a heart-rate biofeedback application. Titles only — we haven't read the claims. *Our call:* Mostly incremental, probably shipping as software updates.

**Meta — silent-speech smart glasses** 🟡
*What:* US 2026/0253591 A1 (priority Feb 2026): combines muscle activity, facial vibration and motion to let glasses read input without speaking aloud, per a patent-analysis blog. *Our call:* Slow; sensors are hard to make comfortable.

## The Schematic, Decoded — Google's "remember what I looked at" glasses

Picture it as three steps:

1. **The gaze trigger.** The glasses aren't recording a movie. They save a frame only when your eyes linger on something for a set time — like a friend who only takes a photo when you say "oh, look at that."
2. **The tidy-up.** Before saving, the glasses throw out blurry and duplicate frames and (per the patent) can mask faces, passwords and nearby people's audio — like a photo album that blurs the strangers.
3. **The ask.** Later, you say "where did I leave my keys?" and the AI searches the saved frames for the last time you stared at them.

Everything useful and everything worrying lives in step 2: how well the tidy-up works decides whether this is a helpful memory or a bystander recorder.
