### 1. Microsoft — "Borrow the world's idle computers"
- **Who:** Microsoft (application published Nov 5, 2026 — US-20260349820-A1).
- **What, plain:** A way to chop a giant AI training job into tiny pieces and run them on the unused power in regular people's computers, possibly paying those people a little.
- **Built before:** Azure (their whole cloud-rental empire), Copilot, years of large-scale AI infrastructure. This flips their model — instead of renting *you* power, they rent power *from* you.
- **Placeholder or Real?** 🟢 **Real, eventually.** Too much money in not building another chip warehouse. Watch the fine print on what "access to your machine" means.
- **Term you learned:** *distributed compute* — one big job, spread across many small machines.

### 2. Jumptuit — "The AI that predicts your health risk"
- **Who:** Jumptuit (patent GRANTED this week — US 12,793,535).
- **What, plain:** An AI that forecasts your personal health risks from your environment — location, exposures, surroundings.
- **Built before:** Earlier Jumptuit AI patents around search and data-correlation. They're stacking health data on top of an existing data-analysis base.
- **Placeholder or Real?** 🟡 **Real tech, drawer timing.** It works, but "we predict your health from your data" wakes up every regulator in the building. Legal swamp before launch.
- **Why you should care personally:** it runs on your most private data, in someone's cloud. That trade should be *your* choice.

### 3. The trend to watch — Agentic AI land grab
- **Who:** Google (Alphabet) and Nvidia, among the first movers.
- **What, plain:** Patents for AI that doesn't just answer you — it *does* things for you (books the flight, not just suggests it).
- **Context:** U.S. AI patent filings went ~1,185 (2023) → ~4,380 (2025). Nearly doubling yearly. The patent office, once skeptical, is now waving AI claims through.
- **Placeholder or Real?** 🟢 **Real and accelerating.** The research paper of today is in your hands in ~18 months.

## 🔧 Schematic, Decoded (patrons-only deep dive)
**This week: Microsoft's distributed-compute diagram.**
Picture it as a mail room. One enormous letter (the AI training job) is too big to deliver. The system shreds it into thousands of postcards (micro-tasks), mails each to a different idle computer, each computer scribbles its small answer, and a central desk staples all the postcards back into one finished letter. The patent's clever bit is the *stapling* — making sure the pieces come back correct and in order even when some computers are slow, offline, or lying. That "trust but verify" step is the whole invention.
