## The full list

**NVIDIA — games that read your mood and adjust** 🟡 *(Patent of the Week)*
*Who:* NVIDIA. *What:* US 2026/0346145 A1, published Nov 5, 2026; filed July 14, 2026 as a continuation of application 18/225,501 (July 2023). A game uses sensors — face, voice, heart and breathing rate, keyboard/mouse behavior, failure history — and neural networks to guess frustration, boredom or fear, then adjusts: easier when frustrated, harder when bored, brighter lighting when too scared. It first identifies the game's genre, since fear in a horror game is the point but in a puzzle game is a problem. Changes may be made with or without telling the player; processing may run on a PC, console or server. Twenty claims; three named inventors; awaiting examination as of July 31. *Built before:* NVIDIA has a separate application for guessing emotion from speech (covered by The Daily Upside, Jan 2024). Games that adjust difficulty from play behavior alone already exist. *Our call:* The behavior-only version likely ships; the face-and-pulse version is slow. The filing gives no accuracy figures.

**Samsung (with Google) — "Intelligent Eyewear" smart glasses** 🟢 *(The Long Game)*
*Who:* Samsung, Google (Android XR), with Gentle Monster and Warby Parker frames. *What:* Camera, microphone and speaker glasses with the Gemini assistant (navigation, translation, photos, calls); no display. Unveiled May 19, 2026; U.S. pre-orders via Warby Parker open Nov 12; deliveries reportedly mid-December, U.S. and South Korea first. Price unconfirmed in anything we found. *Built before:* One report traces Samsung's smart-glasses ambitions to a "Gear Blink" trademark filing in 2014 — a trademark, not a patent or product — about twelve years. *Our call:* Already shipping.

**Samsung — smart-glasses hinge** 🟡
*What:* US 2025/0347929 A1, "Augmented Reality Wearable Electronic Device." A hinge with pulleys linked by a wire so both sides move together, reducing slipping and helping balance during gesture use. Per a Nov 2025 article; we couldn't find the filing date. *Our call:* Unclear if Samsung will use this design. Hinges are boring and necessary; we'd say it's plausible, but it's a guess.

**Lumu — AI that responds to cyberattacks on its own** 🟢
*What:* US 12,712,891 B2, announced Sept 25, 2026 (older than our window). Covers the automated response design behind Lumu Autopilot: cutting off network segments, blocking traffic, changing firewall rules, installing patches, notifying admins; serious incidents go to a human. *Built before:* Autopilot launched in 2024; Lumu reports 19.4 million workflows completed without human intervention (company claim, unverified). *Our call:* Already shipping.

**XTEND — drone control when the connection is weak** 🟢
*What:* US 12,461,522: lets a drone hold its course and targeting under degraded or high-latency communications. Reported in a June 2026 article; we couldn't pin down the grant date. XTEND is set to merge with JFB Construction Holdings and rename itself XTEND AI Robotics (per the article). *Our call:* Ships; defense customers are a ready market.

**Arrive AI — shared package box for drones, robots and couriers** 🟡
*What:* US 12,591,840 (reported April 6, 2026; the company's tenth U.S. patent). One "Arrive Point" holds packages for several homes or businesses, with automated sorting and custody tracking across hand-offs. *Our call:* Slow — it needs both lots of boxes and lots of autonomous delivery.

**Polar — three wearable applications** 🟡
*What:* Published Oct 29: US 2026/0341178 A1 (warm-up coaching), US 2026/0334826 A1 (SpO2 emitter arrangement), US 2026/0334874 A1 (circadian rhythm adaptation). Titles only — we have not read the claims. *Our call:* Probably software and incremental hardware updates.

**Google — wearable with improved biometric sensing** 🟡
*What:* US 2026/0334877 A1, published Oct 29. Title only — no details. *Our call:* Slow; too thin to say more.

**Apple — secure audio indicators for glasses** 🟡 *(carried context from Ep3)*
*What:* "Secure Audio Indicators For Electronic Devices," filed Jan 29, published Aug 13, 2026 — an audio cue connected to smart glasses recording (per VR.org; older than our window). A report from August says Apple's first glasses are expected around WWDC 2027, with the camera decision not yet final (one reporter's account). *Our call:* Ships only if Apple ships glasses.

**Figure AI — humanoid-robot data collection with wearable exoskeleton and gloves** 🟡
*What:* A patent granted in July 2026 (older than our window) for operators to control a humanoid in real time via a wearable exoskeleton and sensor gloves, named Patent of the Month for August by a law firm. *Our call:* Likely a tool inside Figure's own training pipeline rather than a product for you.

**Wearable Devices Ltd. — gesture plus biometric authentication** 🟡
*What:* A continuation patent announced Aug 11, 2026 (older): pairs gesture sensors with biopotential sensors so a gesture can be tied to a specific user, for things like secure payments (company's claim). *Our call:* Slow; niche.

## The Schematic, Decoded — NVIDIA's mood-reading game

Picture it as three steps:

1. **The senses.** The game gathers clues about you: how your face looks, how you sound, your pulse and breathing, how hard you're mashing the keys, and how many times you've failed in the same place. Think of a friend sitting next to you on the couch — except this friend never blinks.
2. **The genre check.** Before reacting, the game figures out what kind of game it is. Same feeling, different meaning: a scream in a horror game is the whole point; a scream in a puzzle game means something's off. It's like a waiter who knows the difference between a diner at a funeral and one at a party.
3. **The nudge.** If your "frustration score" crosses a threshold — possibly after several failures — the game eases off. If you're bored, it ramps up. If fear is too high, maybe the lights come up. And the filing says it may do all this with or without telling you.

Step 3 is the useful part, and step 1 is the sensitive part: everything depends on whether the sensing stays on your device and whether you get a say.
